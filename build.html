You can move down a track at any time. Tracks 1 and 2 can enter two of the three challenges, track 3 all three: Submit and win.

A worked example: Battery Race, an app we built to test track 3: the worked-example folder. Its docs/how-coco-helped.md shows one way to save CoCo's answers. Learn from it, but do not submit it or copy its code.

Rules for every track

  1. Gemma does a real job each time someone uses the app: it answers a question about the data, explains a chart in plain words, or reads a photo. One Gemma call per action is fine; a loop over a whole table is not.
  2. Repo first, in the first 15 minutes: a public GitHub repo with the MIT licence. MIT means anyone may reuse your code if they keep your name and the licence with it. Upload as you go.
  3. Keys and data you may not publish stay out of the repo. For Snowflake data, upload your queries, never the rows.
  4. Your README says: what the app does; the model (Gemma 4 through the Gemini API, with a link to its licence, Apache 2.0); your data (name, source, licence, credit line); how CoCo helped (track 3); the AI tools you used; how to run it. A template is in our repo.

Team tips

  • Invite everyone to the repo, so you can all work at once.
  • Agree in ten minutes what you are building and who does what. Roles: Before Friday.
  • One person edits a file at a time.
