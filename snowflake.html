Snowflake how-to

The clicks and commands for track 3 and the Snowflake challenge. What track 3 is: Build.

CoCo (Cortex Code) is Snowflake's AI coding agent. It runs in three places: on the web inside Snowflake, in the CoCo Desktop app, and as the Cortex CLI in a terminal.

1. Sign up (5 minutes)

Use the link in the email: see Before Friday. Confirm your email, then sign in to Snowflake on the web. No payment card is needed.

2. Check it works (5 minutes)

  1. Open a new SQL worksheet and run:

    SELECT CURRENT_USER();

    Note the name it returns: CoCo Desktop signs in with it, not with your email.

  2. Check Snowflake's AI is switched on for your account. In the same worksheet, run:

    SELECT AI_COMPLETE('llama3.1-8b', 'Say hello in five words');

    A short greeting means it is on.

  3. Open CoCo and ask it one short question, for example "What can you help me with?"

If step 2 or 3 says the feature is not available for trial accounts, ask on the chapter Discord (link on Home). Do not add a payment card. You can still build tracks 1 and 2.

3. Pick where to use CoCo

  • On the web: nothing to install. It cannot see files on your laptop.
  • CoCo Desktop (Mac with Apple silicon, Windows): download it from Snowflake and sign in with the name from section 2.

  • Cortex CLI (macOS, Linux, Windows with WSL), also usable from Codex or Claude Code:

    curl -qLsS https://ai.snowflake.com/static/cc-scripts/install.sh | sh
cortex --version

    Open a new terminal first if cortex is not found. Then connect it to your event account TBC: connection steps. If you have another Snowflake account, check cortex connections list and name the event connection on each command (--connection=<name>).

To work with files in your project folder, use Desktop or the CLI.

4. Get a free Marketplace dataset

  1. Ask CoCo to find a free Marketplace listing for your idea in your account's region. For South Australia, try Data Army Intel's free listings (SEIFA, geography, health care, education, energy and more).
  2. Open the listing and check it says Free yourself: CoCo can be wrong about this.
  3. Click Get and read the terms; accepting them is your call. If Snowflake emails that the listing is being prepared for your region, wait for that email, then click Get again.
  4. Ask CoCo to list the tables and explain the columns. Check its example queries before you trust them.

5. Query it

Ask CoCo for the queries your app needs and run them. Keep each query in a file in your project; the rows stay out of the repo (see Build). Your app reads the results; Gemma is still the AI your users see.

6. Save what CoCo did

At the end of a session, ask CoCo:

Summarise this session for our repo: each thing we asked you, what you found or explained in one or two sentences, and the key query. Leave out account names and data rows.

Save the answer in your repo, for example as a file in a docs folder. The clicks: GitHub how-to.
