Snowflake setup

For Track 3 and the Snowflake challenge: create your account, check it works, and connect CoCo. Do it the evening the sign-up email arrives. The screenshots come from a test account on 28 October; your names and values will differ, and Snowflake may move a label.

1. Create your account (10 minutes)

  1. Open the sign-up link in your email (see Before Friday). Enter your name and an email address you can open now: Snowflake sends an activation link there. Click Continue.
  2. Enter a company name and a job title. Keep the edition, cloud and region the form shows. Read the terms; accepting them is your call. Click Get started. No payment card is needed.
  3. Snowflake asks which languages you use and what you will use it for. Answer, or click Skip.
  4. Open the activation email (it can take a few minutes) and click its link. If the page does not load, try another browser.
  5. Choose a username and a password, then click Get started. Snowflake opens on Home.

2. Check the AI is on (5 minutes)

Open a new SQL worksheet and run:

SELECT AI_COMPLETE('llama3.1-8b', 'Say hello in five words');

A short greeting means it is on. If it says the feature is not available for trial accounts, ask on the chapter Discord (link on Home). Do not add a payment card. You can still build Tracks 1 and 2.

3. Find your connection details

  1. Click your name at the bottom left, then Account, then View Account Details.
  2. The Account tab lists Account identifier and Login name, each with a copy button. You need both to connect CoCo. Use Account identifier (with a hyphen), not Data sharing account identifier (with a dot).

4. Pick where to use CoCo

  • On the web: nothing to install. It cannot see files on your laptop.
  • CoCo Desktop (Mac with Apple silicon, Windows): download it from Snowflake. In Sign in to Snowflake, paste Account identifier into Account Identifier and Login name into Username. Method: Local OAuth. Click Sign In, then sign in on the page that opens in your browser. Back in Desktop, your new connection shows at the top left.
  • Cortex CLI (macOS, Linux, Windows with WSL), also usable from Codex or Claude Code:
    curl -qLsS https://ai.snowflake.com/static/cc-scripts/install.sh | sh
cortex --version

    Open a new terminal first if cortex is not found. Then connect it: section 5.

To work with files in your project folder, use Desktop or the CLI.

5. Connect the Cortex CLI

Connect Desktop first (section 4); in our test the CLI then listed the same connection. Without Desktop, press a to add one with the same Account identifier and Login name. We have not tested that path.

  1. Start cortex in your project folder. If it asks whether to trust the folder, read it first. Type /connections.
  2. Use the arrow keys to highlight your event connection and press s. Then press a to use it in all future sessions, or s for this session only. If a browser page opens, sign in there.
  3. Press Escape. On the main screen, Agent Connection and SQL Connection should both show your event account.

6. Check CoCo can answer and query

Paste this into CoCo (we tested it in the CLI):

Check my current Snowflake connection using read-only queries.

Show the current account, user, role and warehouse.
List available warehouses. If none is selected, use an existing
X-Small warehouse for this session only. Do not create or resize one.

Then run SELECT 1 AS connection_ok.

Do not change data, permissions or persistent settings.

Look for CONNECTION_OK with the value 1. This shows CoCo can answer and run a query; it does not show how much AI use your account has left.

Tested with Cortex Code CLI 1.2.24. Next, get a Marketplace dataset: Snowflake how-to.
